## 1. Conditional mapped-cohort model

Let I(g)=(4π)^−1∫g(n)dΩ on the unit sphere. In a fixed birth frame let

    q(n)=1+nᵀ S n,
    S=Sᵀ, tr S=0, λ_min(S)≥−1.

Then q≥0 and I(q)=1. Strict positivity can be required by an implementation,
but the proof also admits isolated/angular zero sets. Let R be a real
invertible 3×3 map with det R>0, v=Rn and r=|v|. It need not be symmetric, nor
commute with S. Its two frame indices must be carried correctly: R maps birth
physical momentum components to the chosen common final observer frame.

The shape moments are

    F=I(q r),                 P=I(q vvᵀ/r),
    T=diag(F,P),              π=P−F I₃/3.

Here diag denotes a block diagonal 4×4 matrix; T0i=0 follows from antipodal
symmetry. P is positive semidefinite and tr P=F. These identities are exact
for the continuum and any positive antipodally symmetric rule.

For physical cohort birth density n_b and energy E_b, use momentum
p=(E_b/c)Rn and proper-number density dilution D=det R when R is the
admitted homogeneous linear redshift map. The physical tensor factor is
E_b n_b D. The actual BASS weights are proportional to n_b D A_j q(n_j);
E=c|p| enters exactly once through the moment. R is a supplied mapped-cohort
input, not a proof that an arbitrary matrix is generated by an Einstein IVP.
This construction creates no invariant event rate, Source/U/J/PiRate, bath,
native incident face trace, or finite absorption transaction.

## 2. Positive product rule and exact second moment

For N polar Gauss–Legendre nodes μ_i∈(−1,1), weights w_i>0 summing to2, and
M=2N azimuths φ_j=2πj/M, let

    n_ij=(sqrt(1−μ_i²)cosφ_j, sqrt(1−μ_i²)sinφ_j, μ_i),
    A_ij=w_i/(2M),
    Q(g)=Σ_ij A_ij g(n_ij).

Thus Q is positive, normalized and antipodally symmetric. It integrates
every spherical polynomial of degree at most2N−1 exactly in ideal arithmetic.
For a Cartesian monomial of total degree d≤2N−1, its azimuthal Fourier modes
have magnitude at most d<M. The uniform azimuthal sum therefore kills every
nonzero mode. A nonzero constant mode requires even x and y exponents; its
remaining dependence is a polynomial in μ of degree at most d. The N-point
Gauss rule integrates that polynomial exactly. Linearity proves the claim.

The spherical second/fourth moments give

    H=I(q nnᵀ)=I₃/3+2S/15,
    M₂=I(q vvᵀ)=R H Rᵀ,
    m₂=tr M₂=I(q r²).

For N≥3, degree4 exactness also gives Q(q)=1 and Q(q vvᵀ)=M₂.
All eigenvalues of H are at least1/5 under the stated q≥0, trace-free
quadrupole assumptions, so M₂ is positive definite for invertible R.

## 3. Binomial approximation and degree accounting

Let a=σ_min(R)>0, b=σ_max(R),

    s=(a²+b²)/2,
    δ=(b²−a²)/(b²+a²)∈[0,1),
    z(n)=nᵀ(RᵀR/s−I₃)n.

Then |z|≤δ and r=sqrt(s)sqrt(1+z). For p=±1/2 truncate
(1+z)^p after degree K≥0. Since the absolute binomial coefficients decrease,
the uniform remainder obeys

    t_p(K,δ)=|binom(p,K+1)| δ^(K+1)/(1−δ).

For all K≥0,

    t_+(K,δ)=t_−(K,δ)/(2K+1).

The energy polynomial q·Σ_{k≤K}binom(1/2,k)z^k has degree≤2K+2.
Each pressure polynomial q v_i v_j Σ_{k≤K}binom(−1/2,k)z^k has
degree≤2K+4. Thus the sufficient product rule is

    N=K+3, M=2N, number of rays=2(K+3)².

This is a sufficient construction, not a minimum-node theorem. At δ=0 the
tails vanish and N=3 integrates these cohort moments exactly in ideal
arithmetic. Do not evaluate 0·∞ or subtract near-equal logarithms there.

## 4. Source-aware matrix remainder enclosure

Let ΔF=Q(qr)−F and ΔP=Q(qvvᵀ/r)−P. Polynomial exactness cancels each
truncated part. Positivity and I(q)=Q(q)=1 imply

    |ΔF| ≤ B_F = 2 sqrt(s) t_+.

The two appears because both the quadrature remainder and the integral
remainder must be bounded. A one-sided truncation bound alone is insufficient.

For pressure define the inverse-square-root remainder e(n), satisfying
|e(n)|≤t_−/sqrt(s). Since q vvᵀ is positive semidefinite pointwise,

    −(t_−/sqrt(s)) M₂ ≤ Q(q vvᵀ e) ≤ (t_−/sqrt(s)) M₂,
    −(t_−/sqrt(s)) M₂ ≤ I(q vvᵀ e) ≤ (t_−/sqrt(s)) M₂.

Subtracting gives the Loewner enclosure

    −β M₂ ≤ ΔP ≤ β M₂,       β=2t_−/sqrt(s).

This is stronger than replacing vvᵀ by b²I₃ before averaging. In particular,

    ||ΔP||₂ ≤ β ||M₂||₂,
    ||ΔP||_F ≤ β ||M₂||_F,
    |ΔP_ij| ≤ β sqrt((M₂)_ii (M₂)_jj).

For the Frobenius step, write ΔP=β M₂^(1/2) C M₂^(1/2), where C is real
symmetric and ||C||₂≤1. In an eigenbasis of M₂, with eigenvalues m_i,

    ||ΔP/β||_F² = Σ_ij m_i m_j |C_ij|²
       ≤ 1/2 Σ_ij (m_i²+m_j²)|C_ij|²
       ≤ Σ_i m_i².

The last inequality uses squared row and column norms of C at most1. No
commutation of S and RᵀR is assumed. Matrix enclosure also implies
|tr ΔP|≤β tr M₂; since tr ΔP=ΔF, the smaller of this trace bound and B_F
may be reported for energy. The direct binomial B_F is generally sharper.

The STF projection is orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product. Consequently

    ||Δπ||_F²=||ΔP||_F²−(ΔF)²/3 ≤||ΔP||_F².

One does not need to add an extra B_F/3 term for this norm. For a componentwise
max or operator norm use the appropriate different bound; do not transfer the
Frobenius contraction statement without qualification.

For the full rest-frame 4×4 tensor define the Euclidean Frobenius error. It is
invariant under common spatial orthogonal frame rotations, not under arbitrary
Lorentz boosts. The block structure gives

    ||ΔT||_F ≤ B_T = sqrt(B_F² + β² ||M₂||_F²).

All these are ideal-node mathematical enclosures. Actual binary64 nodes,
eigenvalues, coefficients, evaluation and summation need separate numerical
error assessment or rigorous directed-rounding enclosures.

## 5. Source-aware continuum-energy denominator

For y∈[a²,b²], concavity of sqrt yields the chord inequality

    sqrt(y) ≥ (y+ab)/(a+b).

Applying positive normalized I(q·) gives

    F ≥ F_lo=(m₂+ab)/(a+b) ≥ a.

Also F≤sqrt(m₂) by Jensen, so the exact source second moment supplies a
useful bracket. Both bounds have the correct common-scale behavior. For a=b,
F_lo=a=F exactly. The bound is valid even if S and RᵀR do not commute.

The a priori relative full-tensor bound is therefore

    η_T(K)=B_T/F_lo
      = [2t_−/F_lo] sqrt(s/(2K+1)² + ||M₂||_F²/s).

A degree selector can return the first K in a frozen allowed integer range
with η_T(K)≤ε_angular, then construct N=K+3, M=2N positive rays.
This is minimal K within this particular sufficient bound and product-rule
family; it is not the globally minimum quadrature. If the frozen cap is
exhausted, return explicit UNSUPPORTED/BOUND_NOT_MET. Do not clip q, replace
R, relax ε or silently claim success from consecutive-grid agreement.

## 6. Positive mixtures and frame-covariant bookkeeping

For a finite set of already specified cohorts a, let c_a≥0 be physical
positive factors E_b,a n_b,a D_a expressed in the same units and current
observer frame. For each cohort compute T_a, a bound B_T,a and F_lo,a.
Then

    T_total=Σ_a c_a T_a,
    ||ΔT_total||_F ≤ Σ_a c_a B_T,a,
    F_total ≥ Σ_a c_a F_lo,a.

Thus a per-cohort relative bound B_T,a≤ε F_lo,a implies the same ε bound
for the positive sum. Alternatively allocate nonnegative absolute budgets
e_a with Σe_a≤ε Σc_aF_lo,a and require c_a B_T,a≤e_a. A zero-weight
cohort contributes zero and needs no rays. An empty/zero-total mixture has
zero tensor; its relative error is undefined and must be recorded as such.

For independent orthogonal birth/current basis changes O_b,O_f, use

    S′=O_b S O_bᵀ,  R′=O_f R O_bᵀ, n′=O_b n,
    M₂′=O_f M₂ O_fᵀ, P′=O_f P O_fᵀ.

Singular values, F, F_lo, Frobenius/Loewner bounds and selected K are
unchanged. Co-rotated numerical rays satisfy tensor covariance algebraically.
Holding canonical rays fixed while rotating the physical input tests a
different orientation error, which is still covered by the orientation-free
ideal bound. Rotating S alone changes the physical source and is not an
invariance test.

The mixture proof closes an angular error budget for supplied endpoint maps.
Actual B3c history binding still requires each birth S, R, cohort coefficient,
frame, clock/support and identity. It does not certify coupled-geometry
feedback or the accuracy of time integration when the angular rule changes.
