## 1. Physical normalization and the shape integrals

Let `<f>` denote `(4*pi)^-1 integral_S2 f(n) dOmega`. At birth, a massless photon
cohort has proper number density n_b, line energy E_b, normalized angular measure

    d n_b(n) = n_b q_b(n) dOmega/(4*pi),
    q_b(n) = 1 + n^T S_b n,
    S_b = S_b^T, tr(S_b)=0, lambda_min(S_b)>-1.

Thus `<q_b>=1` and the measure is strictly positive. The source-frame occupation
or finite incident-face trace is not specified by this measure. It is an already
declared finite photon cohort, not a new absorption donor or native REC history.

Use one prescribed log-expansion a and tracefree anisotropic log-expansion B:

    C_eta = exp(-eta B),     R_eta = exp(-a) C_eta,
    D = exp(-3a),            rho_b = E_b n_b.

B is diagonal for the aligned fixtures. R maps birth physical momenta to final
physical momenta; D maps proper number density. With `v=C_eta n` and `r=|v|`,

    n_final = D n_b,
    rho_final = rho_b D exp(-a) F,
    P_final = rho_b D exp(-a) P,
    F = <q_b r>,
    P = <q_b v v^T/r>,
    pi_shape = P - (F/3) I.

Odd flux is zero by antipodal symmetry. The common factor is physical energy
density in J m^-3. It cancels from relative angular errors. Every construction
obeys `tr(P)=F` pointwise before angular integration: it is the massless stress
trace identity, not an accuracy test that can detect all angular bias.

For a finite positive rule `(n_j,A_j)`, use q_b at the **birth** n_j. Do not
reevaluate it at the transported direction `C_eta n_j/|C_eta n_j|`. The actual
BASS state representation is

    k_j = [E_b/(c Pstar)] exp(-a) C_eta n_j,
    w_j = alphaL c Pstar n_b D A_j q_b(n_j),
    mass_j = 0,
    E_final,j = c Pstar |k_j|.

Here alphaL=kappa L^2 is fixed if BASS dimensionless packet routines are used.
Weights are state proper-density weights. No state Doppler rule is applied to
invariant event rates, and no Source/U/J/PiRate is generated by this one-cohort
comparison.

At eta=0, for any admitted S_b,

## 3. Q14 leading transported-moment errors

These coefficient formulas assume B is diagonal in the canonical cubature axes.
They are not universal orientation-independent bias functionals for a fixed
unrotated cubature. Write

    t=n^T B n,   u=n^T B^2 n,   s=n^T S_b n.

For small eta,

    |exp(-eta B)n| = 1 - eta t + eta^2 (u-t^2/2) + O(eta^3),
    1/|exp(-eta B)n| = 1 + eta t + eta^2(-u+3t^2/2) + O(eta^3).

The unweighted terms and q_b-times-linear terms are degree<=4. The first
source-anisotropy energy defect is consequently

    Delta14 F = -(eta^2/2) Delta14[s t^2] + O(eta^3)
              = -(eta^2/35) sum_i (S_b)_ii B_i^2 + O(eta^3).

Off-diagonal entries of S_b do not contribute to F for diagonal transport,
because their integrands are odd under one coordinate reflection. A nonzero S_b
does not by itself guarantee a nonzero displayed coefficient; cancellations
must be respected.

The first spatial-tensor defect is

    Delta14 P_ii
       = (2 eta/105)[3(S_b)_ii B_i - tr(S_b B)] + O(eta^2),
    Delta14 P_ij
       = -(4 eta/105)(B_i+B_j)(S_b)_ij + O(eta^2),  i!=j.

The leading tensor has zero trace, consistent with the energy defect beginning
one order later. The same leading term is the STF-stress defect. Generic
source-anisotropic energy and STF-stress errors thus begin at orders2 and1.

For isotropic birth emission S_b=0,

    Delta14 F = -(eta^3/35) tr(B^3) + O(eta^4),
    Delta14 P_ii = (eta^2/35)[3B_i^2-tr(B^2)] + O(eta^3),
    Delta14 P_ij = 0,  i!=j.

These follow from the next terms `-t^3/2` in the square-root expansion and
`3t^2/2` in the inverse-square-root expansion. Degenerate B can cancel the cubic
energy coefficient. In particular B=(b,-b,0) has tr(B^3)=0, so it is unsuitable
for demonstrating a generic cubic energy error.

## 6. Bounded continuum reference from positive product quadrature

Use mu=n_z in[-1,1] and uniform phi in[0,2pi), with positive Gauss-Legendre
weights in mu and equal positive weights in phi. The normalized sphere average
has weights `w_mu/(2 N_phi)`. For N_mu=n and N_phi=2n, this product rule
integrates every spherical polynomial of total degree<=2n-1 exactly in ideal
arithmetic: the phi sum annihilates nonzero Fourier modes below N_phi, and the
remaining mu polynomial is within the Gauss exactness degree.

There is a direct analytic truncation bound for its use on these nonpolynomial
cohort integrands. Let a_min,a_max be the smallest/largest eigenvalues of C^2,

    a0=(a_max+a_min)/2,
    z(n)=n^T(C^2/a0-I)n,
    delta=(a_max-a_min)/(a_max+a_min)<1.

Then |z|<=delta. Truncate `(1+z)^p` at degreeK, p=+1/2 or-1/2. Since the
absolute binomial coefficients decrease,

    tail_p(K,delta)
       <= abs(binomial(p,K+1))*delta^(K+1)/(1-delta).

The source-weighted energy polynomial has degree<=2K+2; the source-weighted
spatial-tensor polynomial has degree<=2K+4. Thus K=n-3 is sufficient for both
with N_phi=2n. Since q_b is positive and both the exact integral and the product
rule integrate q_b to1, the ideal-node quadrature errors obey

    abs(F_reference-F_continuum) <= 2 sqrt(a0) tail_(+1/2),
    max_ij abs(P_reference,ij-P_continuum,ij)
        <= 2 r_max^2/sqrt(a0) tail_(-1/2).

The factor2 bounds separately the integral and quadrature remainders. For a
relative tensor bound divide by F_continuum>=r_min. The STF bound additionally
includes one third of the energy bound. The proof also holds for a rotated SPD
C, because z is still a quadratic polynomial and
`|(Cn)_i(Cn)_j|<=r_max^2`.

For the main eta<=1 spectrum, delta<=tanh(1/2). The n64,phi128 reference permits
K61 and therefore a negligible ideal-node truncation error compared with2e-12.
This is an analytic approximation bound, **not** an interval enclosure of the
actual binary64 Gauss nodes, weights, transcendental evaluations or summation.
Observed n16/32/64 agreement, independent octant and axial checks assess those
floating calculations without relabeling them as certified bounds.

For aligned diagonal S_b,C, each diagonal/energy integrand is even under every
coordinate reflection. An independent octant integral is

    (2/pi) integral_0^1 dmu integral_0^(pi/2) dphi f(n).

Two32-point Gauss-Legendre rules on these different intervals provide a useful
independent numerical check. The octant reduction must not be used unchanged

An optional independently exact polynomial-moment evaluation uses

## 7. Axial isotropic-birth closed form

For S_b=0 and C=diag(r_perp,r_perp,r_parallel), define
`A=r_perp^2`, `d=r_parallel^2-r_perp^2>0`. Direct integration over mu gives

    F = r_parallel/2
        + A asinh(sqrt(d/A))/(2 sqrt(d)),
    P_zz = r_parallel^2/(2d)

    P_xx=P_yy=(F-P_zz)/2.

This is an independent asinh oracle. At d=0 use the exact limits F=r_perp and
P_ii=r_perp/3; do not subtract nearly equal finite expressions to manufacture an

The closed form does not replace the non-axial continuum checks; it catches a

## 8. Rotation and orientation are different checks

For an orthogonal O, simultaneously replace

    C -> O C O^T,   S_b -> O S_b O^T,   n_j -> O n_j.

Then q_b is unchanged on each labelled ray, Cn rotates as a vector, scalar F is
unchanged and P transforms to O P O^T. This is true for the exact continuum and
for either finite rule, regardless of its angular accuracy. It is the relevant
co-rotated-ray covariance check.

If the physical pair C,S_b is rotated but the canonical quadrature directions
are held fixed, the continuum scalar is unchanged and its tensor rotates, while
the finite approximation can change its error. That measures orientation
sensitivity of the angular rule. It must not be described as a violation of
tensor covariance. Rotating S_b alone with C fixed instead changes the physical
cohort and is not an invariance test at all.

The proposed rational rotation with xy block[[3/5,-4/5],[4/5,3/5]] is a proper
orthogonal matrix and is adequate for these two separate checks. Neither Q14 nor
Q26 has full rotational symmetry as a finite point set.
